# Product ranking interview review

**My opening statement**

“I agree with ranking a fixed set of products per query and starting with a tabular ranker. Before I fit anything, I want to confirm what counts as relevance and which search data I can use. I can already demonstrate that the evaluator works on the supplied CSV.”

This review uses the interviewer’s prompt as the contract. The two drafts under review are the [original specification](../specs/product-ranking-model.md) and [development plan](product-ranking-model.dev-plan.md). A [revised interview specification](../specs/product-ranking-interview-spec.md) already incorporates several corrections.

## 2. Definite errors I would correct directly

These are statements the prompt, repository, arithmetic, library documentation, or the drafts’ own requirements settle.

| Location and draft claim | What I would say |
|---|---|
| Spec §1, §4 and plan §0 make purchase the objective supplied by product. | “The interviewer asked for the most **relevant** products first and supplied five grades. Purchase is the highest grade. Making purchase MRR override graded relevance adds a product decision. I’ll use graded relevance as the stated objective and ask who decides any trade-off.” |
| Spec §2 and §4 remove dwell, define grade 1 as any click, and reserve grade 2 for wishlist. | “The brief explicitly defines grade 1 as a low-dwell click and grade 2 as a high-dwell click **or** wishlist. I’ll read `relevance_grade` from the CSV. Its three binary labels cannot reconstruct that distinction.” |

For the usual definition, identical nonzero vectors give 0 and opposite vectors give 2. A valid value of 1.4 would be discarded by this cleaner. I’ll test a known pair through the producer. ## 3. Plausible choices I would clarify with the interviewer

**I’d ask the first five before coding.** The remaining questions belong at the point where their answer would change the next step.

| Topic | Question I would ask aloud | What I’d do with the answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Goal and success | “Suppose one model surfaces better query matches and increases wishlists, while another increases purchases. Which result would you prefer? Do we have human relevance judgments?” | Start with query-level graded NDCG. Agree on the online primary outcome and whether conversion, revenue, or satisfaction is a guardrail. |
| 2 · Exercise and data | “What would you like working by the end of this pairing session? Is a larger timestamped search export available?” | Demonstrate a loader, baselines, and metric tests on the fixture. Move to real training, confidence intervals, serving, and experimentation when the required data and scope exist. |
| 3 · Request and exposure | “Does one `qid` mean one page, one search across pages, or one reranking call? Can I see every retrieved candidate or only what was shown?” | Set the group key, evaluation universe, pagination/tie rules, and group-feature population. Unshown items have unknown outcomes. A visible item is evidence of an opportunity to examine it. |
| 4 · Label semantics | “How long is high dwell? What happens when the dwell timer is missing, or a wishlist, cart, or purchase arrives without a click?” | Respect supplied grades. For future logs, document event precedence, visibility, missingness, and the conditions under which `clicked=0` implies grade 0. |
| 5 · Attribution | “An item appears in two searches and is bought the following day. Which impression receives credit? Do cross-session purchases count?” | Prefer direct links and deduplicate order lines. Agree on an attribution window, a repeat-exposure rule, maturity cutoffs, and return handling. In the feed example, 1,719 of 3,353 linked purchases arrived after 24 hours. Search-specific rates require search logs. |
| Semantic feature | “The brief calls it cosine distance. Can we check the producer on an identical query–item pair, including its behavior for missing or zero vectors?” | Test the formula, supported range, sort direction, and missing-value treatment before adding a monotonic constraint. |
| Business weights and cutoffs | “Are the five levels purely ordinal? Which positions matter on mobile and desktop, and should purchases have extra utility beyond their grade?” | Choose a fixed evaluation gain table and cutoff. Sweep training gains on validation if useful, while scoring all contenders with the same evaluation gains. Define multiple-purchase MRR, zero-gain groups, tie handling, and metric coverage. |
| Bias and exploration | “What generated the logged order? Was anything randomized? Does the logged propensity describe item selection, slot assignment, or examination?” | Compare the uncorrected baseline first. A click-derived exposure estimate requires assumptions to transfer to cart and purchase. On the feed branch the stored field is item sampling weight times page size. EM, inverse weighting, clipping at 0.1, and a 1–2% exploration rate each need justification. |
| Group sampling and calibration | “Do we retain all searches, including no-click and all-equal groups? What is the distribution the online metric should describe?” | Keep natural traffic for evaluation and calibration. If only 10% of zero-positive groups are retained for fitting, record inclusion probabilities and account for sampling when estimating population quantities. Equal-grade groups give no pairs for LambdaRank. Other losses can behave differently. |
| Historical features and cold start | “Which features exist at request time? Is AOV known for a new user? Do we have impression denominators, product rating counts, currency, and the entire candidate set?” | Compute CTR and tier medians as of the request, smooth sparse counts, and handle unknown tier, zero variance, missing price, and unequal currencies. Within-group ranks may change when a page is truncated. Test gains from each feature group. |
| Split, inference and statistical unit | “Should this estimate future traffic for returning users, performance on unseen users, or both? Can one user issue multiple dependent requests?” | A chronological split can report both slices. A user-disjoint split measures a different population. Purge boundary-crossing sessions and immature labels, check each partition’s end against the next start, and cluster uncertainty at the appropriate session or user unit. |
| Scale, latency, and fallback | “What are the actual candidate counts, traffic, hardware, feature-fetch budget, and fallback order? Which limits are requirements?” | Measure model-only and end-to-end p50/p99 under representative load. Keep incoming order as a fallback candidate when BM25 is unavailable. Confirm budgets before treating 2/8 ms, 99.9% availability, or a 90-day history as acceptance criteria. |
| Cleaning and retention | “Which ID formats, device types, locales, currency conventions, and retention rules come from the producer? How should we handle duplicate impressions and sensitive query text?” | Keep typed validation and counted exclusions. A pattern inferred from ten rows can discard real IDs, ISBNs, SKUs, or multilingual queries. Stripping a currency symbol loses currency identity. A repeated item can be a real second exposure. Test the entire request after a row drop. |
| Online decision | “What is the minimum worthwhile improvement, which harms are unacceptable, and how much eligible traffic is available? Can we hold retrieval fixed?” | Power the user-randomized experiment for the agreed metric, account for repeated sessions, define intervals and a stopping rule, predeclare any multiple primary gates, and wait for delayed outcomes. Interleaving can be considered for a preference pre-check. |
| Operations and scope | “Is this a design discussion, a prototype, or a launch plan? Do we need retention policy, neural heads, fairness, a deployment API, or a retraining schedule today?” | Document any requested constraint. Defer optional infrastructure, fixed maintenance intervals, and costly tuning until their value and owner are clear. |

### Claims I would soften on my own

- **Model and feature claims, §§4–6.** Purchase prevalence on a historical feed does not establish that direct purchase training is infeasible for search. Sample size, positive pairs, coverage, and measured performance decide. Neither high feature importance nor a modest AUC proves leakage or identifies a feature bottleneck. Verify feature timestamps, do ablations, and measure on the target data. A binary scorer is a valid ranking baseline.
- **Dataset observations, §§2–3.** Positions 1–5 form a permutation and both purchases are at position 1 in this fixture. Future logs need documented exposure semantics. They need not reproduce those correlations or a 20–100 item page size. A click-only log omits the exposure denominator and many potential comparisons. Record the candidate list before scoring, the served order, policy version, dwell, and a stable impression ID. Call the logged order “production” only after verifying its policy.
- **Position correction, §5.4.** Feeding position into a tree and setting it to 1 at serving can create a train–serve mismatch. The tree may learn position interactions. A randomized CTR ratio estimates relative examination only under the chosen click model and comparable item mix. Check device, viewport, confidence intervals, weight concentration, and the available range of positions. For policy evaluation on shuffled traffic, also log the original incumbent order.
- **Model configuration, §6.3–7.3.** Larger auxiliary-loss coefficients alone do not guarantee larger gradient contributions. LightGBM’s native dataset takes contiguous query groups and group **sizes**. Ensure bagging preserves the intended group structure. Tune leaf size, training truncation, stopping metric, and tree count on validation. A 100-row minimum leaf cannot demonstrate a learned split on ten rows. [LightGBM dataset API](https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/stable/pythonapi/lightgbm.Dataset.html) · [Bagging by query](https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/stable/Parameters.html#bagging_by_query)
- **Statistics and benchmarks, §§1, 5, 7–8.** The draft offers +5–10% model lift, 10–20% split inflation, 10× interleaving efficiency, an 8–10% annual conversion ambition, and minutes-to-train without measurements for this task. Equal session counts do not establish equal variance or equal calendar durations. Feature importance above 50%, a raw NDCG value higher than conversion, or correlation across five noisy experiments cannot diagnose leakage or validate a surrogate. Treat these as hypotheses with units, denominators, and evidence.
- **Monitoring and rollout, §§1, 7.4–8.6.** A fixed mean top-ten price does not establish that a model avoided cheap-item bias. Inspect exposures by price band, query mix, and user tier. A model head for returns does not supply actual order-return events. Define the proposed online NDCG proxy and who responds to drift. Pin the training-data snapshot and dependency versions as well as the code SHA. Define what can be learned after four weeks when returns have a 30-day window.
- **Readiness and cleaning, §9.** One million rows, 50,000 groups, 5% feature missingness, type-specific regexes, and fixed grade prevalence are proposals. Estimate usable requests and effective positives. Define denominators for all drop thresholds, which are missing for several rules. Make the numeric parser match the supplier’s serialization. HTML stripping can erase tokens. A URL match says little about a user’s search intent. Test both false positives and false negatives before automatically discarding whole requests. Keep format checks separate from sample-size checks. The proposed Unicode character classes such as `\p{L}` need a compatible engine. Python’s built-in `re` rejects them. Enforce a BM25 range only after checking the retrieval implementation.

## 4. Development plan, milestone by milestone

| Milestone | Keep | Change I would request during pairing |
|---|---|---|
| **M0 · Setup** | Small runnable environment, schema test, lint and tests. | Inspect the existing venv and historical branch. Working fixture code is a better first deliverable than empty package stubs. A live exploration request needs an owner and falls outside the supplied interview task. |
| **M1 · Dataset** | Validate keys and types, count rows and groups, record exclusions. | Read the supplied CSV first. Separate fixture validation from full-data readiness. Document the source for each required search field and label before building an exporter. Keep a versioned output distinct from the fixture. |
| **M2 · Labels and features** | One shared builder with point-in-time tests and within-query features. | Accept supplied grades. Implement event-derived grades only after dwell, attribution, timestamps, and event IDs are available. Test unseen tiers, identical within-group scores, and missing features. |
| **M3 · Evaluator** | Hand-computed NDCG, purchase rank and recall, paired baseline comparison. | Move the small deterministic evaluator up. Test multiple purchases, tied scores, no purchases, and zero ideal gain. Define the averaging unit and report coverage. Run slices, intervals, and bias corrections only on data that supports them. |
| **M4 · Baselines** | Logged order, BM25, popularity, pointwise scorer. | Run deterministic baselines immediately. Freeze splits before fitting the pointwise model. Preserve identical requests, candidates, and gain definitions across comparisons. |
| **M5 · LambdaMART** | Train one grouped model, version it, and exercise train–serve parity. | Make integration success independent of whether the model wins. Use validation for development. The two-session fixture cannot populate three nonempty chronological partitions or satisfy ±1-point share checks. Use a separate synthetic split test. |
| **M6 · Debiasing** | Investigate position effects and version any estimator actually used. | Start with raw diagnostics. Establish search logging support first. Specify the correction for the graded loss and how its evaluation is validated. A randomized-evaluation gate cannot be passed with observational EM estimates alone. |
| **M7 · Selection** | Tune on validation, freeze config, run one final comparison. | Lock the evaluation gains and primary metric before sweeping training gains. Scale the trial budget to data and time. A 90-fit grid from three gains and 30 trials is optional. Record the winning config with its data version. |
| **M8 · Serving** | Score candidates once, test fallback, measure latency, check parity. | Agree on candidate count, load, hardware, feature availability, tie breaks, and fallback order. A scoring-only benchmark omits feature lookup and request overhead. Test a timeout and a missing retrieval score. |
| **M9 · Experiment** | Specify randomization, power, guardrails, ramp and rollback. | Agree on the primary outcome and control baseline before exposure. Define persistent holdback allocation and label maturity. Filing an experiment plan, approving a ramp, and observing business lift are distinct completion conditions. |

**Plan §§0–1 and 3–8.** Keep the module map, named artifacts, dataset facts, risk list, and deliverable mapping. Fill the known fixture facts now and label feed figures separately. Correct the spec’s stale section links for debiasing and offline evaluation. Fix the feature path and map each claimed output to its implementation, including MAP or probability metrics if retained. Use separate definitions of done for the live exercise, a trained candidate, and a launched system. Ten sequential engineer-weeks, ten PRs, an HTML report, and passing a lift target are scope choices. A well-run experiment can also conclude that the incumbent wins.

## 5. Numbers I can defend at the whiteboard

Each score below is mean NDCG over the **same two search requests**. NDCG is bounded by 1. Changing the gain table changes the metric.

| Gain assigned to grades 0–4 | Logged order, NDCG@10 | BM25, NDCG@10 | Maximum possible relative lift over logged order |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Original draft · 0, 1, 2, 5, 20 | 0.99713 | 0.98358 | 0.288% |
| Revised interview spec · 0, 1, 3, 7, 15 | 0.99503 | 0.97312 | 0.500% |

For *wireless headphones*, BM25 moves the unclicked, grade-0 `p_303` ahead of the carted, grade-3 `p_302`. The purchased item stays first. For *mechanical keyboard*, BM25 preserves the logged order. That explains how graded NDCG can fall while purchase MRR stays at **1.0**. Purchase recall@10 is also **1.0** for every ordering of these five-item groups. The original +2% NDCG and +3% MRR gates cannot be demonstrated on this fixture. They require a different evaluation population. [Search fixture](../../data/ranking_items.csv)

**What remains provisional in the revised spec.** The exponential gain table, NDCG@10, incoming-order fallback, item-ID tie break, and proposed online share of sessions with a grade 2–4 outcome are reasonable defaults. That online metric gives the same session-level credit for long dwell and purchase, and long dwell can indicate confusion. Surface these choices to the interviewer. My closing statement would be “With your agreement on the success metric, I’ll reproduce both baseline scores now. Once we have representative search logs, I’ll run a chronological baseline-versus-LambdaMART comparison.”
